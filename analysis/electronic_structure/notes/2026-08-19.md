# Electronic Structure Analysis Notes

## β-Ga₂O₃ Oxygen Vacancies

**Data set:** 2026-08-17 SCF results  
**Systems:** pristine, O1 vacancy, O2 vacancy, O3 vacancy

---

## 1. Current analysis workflow

The current electronic-structure analysis consists of three main steps:

1. Total density of states (DOS)
2. First-shell Ga projected density of states (PDOS)
3. Matched pristine–vacancy Ga-PDOS comparison

The purpose is to understand how oxygen vacancies modify the electronic
structure of β-Ga₂O₃, with particular attention to the Ga atoms directly
neighboring the vacancy.

The current PDOS analysis focuses on Ga s, p, and d orbital contributions.

---

## 2. Total DOS

Total DOS was calculated for:

- pristine β-Ga₂O₃
- O1 vacancy
- O2 vacancy
- O3 vacancy

The energy axis is expressed relative to the Fermi energy:

E - E_F

so that E_F = 0 eV for each structure.

### Preliminary observation

The oxygen-vacancy structures show clear changes in the DOS compared with
the pristine structure, particularly near the band-edge region.

The DOS-derived gap obtained in the current analysis decreases from the
pristine structure to the oxygen-vacancy structures.

Current approximate values:

| Structure | DOS-derived gap (eV) |
|---|---:|
| Pristine | 1.788 |
| O1 vacancy | 1.236 |
| O2 vacancy | 0.912 |
| O3 vacancy | 1.005 |

Among the three vacancy structures, O2 currently shows the largest
reduction in the DOS-derived gap.

These values should be treated as PBE-level / DOS-derived quantities rather
than quantitative experimental band gaps.

---

## 3. First-shell Ga atoms

The PDOS analysis focuses on Ga atoms in the first coordination shell of
each oxygen vacancy.

The atom selection and pristine–defect mapping are stored in:

analysis/electronic_structure/pdos/pdos_targets.csv

Current first-shell Ga sites are:

### O1 vacancy

- tetrahedral Ga: Ga29, Ga25
- octahedral Ga: Ga2

### O2 vacancy

- tetrahedral Ga: Ga17
- octahedral Ga: Ga9, Ga1, Ga5

### O3 vacancy

- tetrahedral Ga: Ga27
- octahedral Ga: Ga13, Ga9

The relaxed structures used to generate the atom mapping have not changed
in the current SCF update. Therefore, the existing first-shell Ga mapping
is retained for the 2026-08-17 SCF results.

---

## 4. Ga-PDOS analysis

For each selected Ga site, the PDOS is decomposed into:

- Ga-s
- Ga-p
- Ga-d

The current plotted energy window is:

-6 eV <= E - E_F <= 6 eV

Strong Ga-d states also occur at deeper energies outside this displayed
window. The plotting routine therefore determines the y-axis scale only
from the visible energy window.

---

## 5. Preliminary pristine–vacancy comparison

Matched Ga atoms in the pristine and vacancy structures were compared
using the atom mapping stored in `pdos_targets.csv`.

### General observations

Oxygen-vacancy formation produces a clear redistribution of the electronic
states of first-shell Ga atoms.

The response is coordination dependent:

- tetrahedral and octahedral Ga sites show different PDOS changes;
- Ga-s and Ga-p contributions show particularly noticeable changes in the
  band-edge region;
- Ga-d states are strong at deeper energies but appear less dominant in the
  currently examined near-edge window.

---

## 6. O1 vacancy

The first-shell Ga atoms are:

- Ga29 and Ga25: tetrahedral
- Ga2: octahedral

Ga29 and Ga25 show very similar PDOS responses, consistent with similar
local environments.

The octahedral Ga2 shows a distinct response from the tetrahedral Ga sites.

A pronounced redistribution of Ga-s-derived states is observed on the
conduction-band side after O1-vacancy formation.

---

## 7. O2 vacancy

The first-shell Ga atoms are:

- Ga17: tetrahedral
- Ga9, Ga1, Ga5: octahedral

The tetrahedral Ga17 shows pronounced changes in s/p character near the
upper valence / band-edge region.

The three octahedral Ga sites show similar PDOS responses to one another,
with particularly noticeable changes in Ga-s-derived states on the
conduction-band side.

This suggests that the electronic response to the O2 vacancy depends
strongly on the local Ga coordination environment.

---

## 8. O3 vacancy

The first-shell Ga atoms are:

- Ga27: tetrahedral
- Ga13 and Ga9: octahedral

The tetrahedral and octahedral Ga sites again show different PDOS responses.

The two octahedral Ga sites exhibit similar behavior, while the
tetrahedral Ga27 shows a distinct near-edge electronic response.

This is qualitatively consistent with the coordination-dependent behavior
observed for O1 and O2 vacancies.

---

## 9. Current interpretation

The current results support the following working picture:

oxygen vacancy
→ local structural perturbation
→ redistribution of first-shell Ga electronic states
→ modification of the total electronic structure

In particular, the first-shell Ga response appears to depend on whether
the Ga site is tetrahedrally or octahedrally coordinated.

This suggests that the electronic effect of an oxygen vacancy cannot be
described only by the removal of an O atom; the local coordination
environment of the neighboring Ga atoms also plays an important role.

---

## 10. Important limitations

The current pristine–vacancy PDOS comparison uses each structure's own
Fermi energy as the zero of energy:

E_plot = E - E_F

Therefore, the current plots are suitable for comparing:

- PDOS shape
- intensity
- orbital character
- qualitative redistribution of electronic states

However, they should NOT yet be used to claim precise absolute energy
shifts between pristine and vacancy structures.

In particular, a horizontal displacement of a PDOS peak should not yet be
interpreted directly as a vacancy-induced energy-level shift.

The current results also do not by themselves prove that a particular PDOS
peak is a vacancy-induced defect state.

---

## 11. Next steps

The next stage should focus on:

1. determining VBM, CBM, and band-gap-related features for pristine and
   vacancy structures;

2. establishing an appropriate energy-alignment strategy for quantitative
   pristine–defect comparison;

3. defining physically meaningful PDOS integration windows near the
   valence- and conduction-band edges;

4. quantifying changes in Ga-s, Ga-p, and Ga-d contributions;

5. correlating the electronic response with local structural descriptors,
   including bond-length and bond-angle changes;

6. comparing O1, O2, and O3 vacancies quantitatively;

7. determining whether specific near-gap states can be identified as
   vacancy-induced defect states.

---

## Working conclusion

At the current stage, the calculations indicate that oxygen vacancies
substantially modify the local electronic structure of neighboring Ga atoms.

The redistribution of Ga-derived states is strongly dependent on the local
coordination environment, with tetrahedral and octahedral Ga sites showing
distinct responses.

Further energy alignment and quantitative PDOS analysis are required before
assigning specific peaks to defect states or making quantitative statements
about vacancy-induced energy-level shifts.
