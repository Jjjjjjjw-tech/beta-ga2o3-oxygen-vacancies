## Objective

Quantitatively analyze the local Ga–O bond-length changes around oxygen vacancies after structural relaxation.

---

## Work Completed

-Developed an automated bond-length analysis workflow.
-Identified first-shell Ga atoms based on vacancy–Ga distances.
-Automatically determined the number of first-shell Ga atoms for each vacancy configuration.
-Matched corresponding Ga atoms between pristine and vacancy structures using nearest-neighbor distance mapping.
-Compared the coordination environment before and after vacancy formation.
-Identified lost Ga–O bonds caused by oxygen removal.
-Calculated bond-length changes for all remaining Ga–O bonds.
-Generated summary CSV files for individual vacancies and the complete dataset.
-Visualized the average Ga–O bond-length changes using a bar chart.

---

## Main Observations

### Vacancy O1

Three first-shell Ga atoms were identified.

Two tetrahedral Ga atoms (Ga29 and Ga25) changed from 4-fold to 3-fold coordination.
Their remaining Ga–O bonds increased by approximately +0.06 Å on average.
One octahedral Ga atom (Ga2) changed from 6-fold to 5-fold coordination.
Its remaining Ga–O bonds became slightly shorter (−0.035 Å).

---

### Vacancy O2

Four first-shell Ga atoms were identified.

One tetrahedral Ga atom (Ga17) showed the largest bond elongation (≈ +0.10 Å).
Three octahedral Ga atoms (Ga9, Ga1, and Ga5) exhibited only small bond contractions.

---

### Vacancy O3

Three first-shell Ga atoms were identified.

One tetrahedral Ga atom (Ga27) displayed significant bond elongation (≈ +0.10 Å).
Two octahedral Ga atoms (Ga13 and Ga9) showed slight bond contractions.
---

## Main Conclusions

A consistent local structural trend is observed for all three oxygen vacancies.

Tetrahedral Ga atoms lose one oxygen neighbor and their remaining Ga–O bonds become longer.
Octahedral Ga atoms also lose one oxygen neighbor, but their remaining Ga–O bonds become slightly shorter.

This indicates that tetrahedral and octahedral Ga atoms respond differently to oxygen-vacancy formation.

## Questions

Why do tetrahedral and octahedral Ga atoms exhibit opposite bond-length relaxation?

Possible factors include:

different local coordination geometry,
different bonding strength,
charge redistribution after vacancy formation,
electronic structure changes.

These mechanisms require further investigation.

## Key Takeaway

Oxygen-vacancy formation produces opposite local relaxation behaviors in tetrahedral and octahedral Ga atoms: the remaining Ga–O bonds of tetrahedral Ga elongate, whereas those of octahedral Ga contract slightly.
